# VIDEO 08: Insurance Claims -- Using RCRS Data to Support Every Claim

**Duration:** 5 minutes
**Audience:** Bart (Insurance Claims Specialist)
**URL:** /portal/sales/customers

---

## NARRATION SCRIPT

[SCREEN: Browser opening rivercityroofingsolutions.com/portal. The login page loads. After entering email and PIN, the portal dashboard appears.]

Welcome to the insurance claims workflow. As the Insurance Claims Specialist, your job is to build the strongest possible case for every claim -- and this system gives you every tool you need to do that. Let me walk you through how to use the platform from the moment a claim lands on your desk to the day it gets paid.

[SCREEN: Navigate to /portal/sales/customers. A list of customer records loads from JobNimbus CRM. Each row shows customer name, phone, email, address, status badge, and assigned sales rep. A search bar is at the top with placeholder text "Search customers..."]

Start at the Customers page. This pulls directly from JobNimbus, our CRM, and syncs both directions -- changes you make here update in JobNimbus and vice versa. Use the search bar to find any customer by name, phone, email, or address. When a sales rep closes a deal that involves an insurance claim, they hand it off to you. Your first step is to pull up that customer's record.

[SCREEN: Type a customer name in the search bar. The list filters to one result. Click the customer name. The customer detail page loads at /portal/sales/customers/[id] showing the customer name, phone, email, full address, status badge, and assigned rep at the top. Six tabs are visible below: Overview, Jobs, Estimates, Tasks, Notes, and Documents.]

The customer detail page is your home base for every claim. It has six tabs that give you a complete picture. Let me walk through each one.

[SCREEN: The Overview tab is active showing full contact information: name, phone numbers (mobile, home, work), email, complete address, status in the pipeline, lead source, assigned sales rep, and record dates.]

The Overview tab has all the contact details. Full name, every phone number on file, email, complete address with city, state, and ZIP. You also see the customer's current status in the pipeline, how they found us, which sales rep is assigned, and when the record was created. When you are coordinating with an adjuster and they need the homeowner's information, this is where you pull it.

[SCREEN: Click the "Jobs" tab. A list of jobs appears showing job number, name, description, status badge (e.g., "In Progress" in blue, "Completed" in green), property address, assigned rep, start date, and end date.]

The Jobs tab shows every project associated with this customer. Each job has a number, description, status, address, the rep handling it, and the start and end dates. For claims, you need the job status and timeline. When was the damage inspected? When was work authorized? When was it completed? These dates matter when the insurance company asks for a project timeline.

[SCREEN: Click the "Estimates" tab. A list of estimates shows estimate number, status badge (Approved, Pending, Rejected), line-item amount, tax, total, and links to PDF download and public URL.]

The Estimates tab is critical for claims work. Every estimate we have written for this customer is here with the full dollar breakdown -- line items, tax, and total. You can download the PDF directly and share it with the adjuster. When the adjuster's scope does not match our estimate, this is where you pull the numbers for your supplement.

[SCREEN: Click the "Notes" tab. A chronological list of notes appears with timestamps, author names (sales rep, office staff, Bart), and note content. A note reads: "Spoke with adjuster -- they are approving shingles and underlayment, pushing back on flashing. Scheduling re-inspection for Friday."]

The Notes tab is your claims journal. Every note written about this customer appears in chronological order. When you have a call with an adjuster, add a note immediately: what was discussed, what documents they requested, what they agreed to, what they pushed back on, and the next step. This creates a paper trail that protects RCRS if there is ever a dispute. Get in the habit of documenting every adjuster interaction here.

[SCREEN: Click the "Documents" tab. A list of uploaded files appears with file names, types (PDF, JPG, PNG), upload dates, and download links. Files include: "Inspection-Photos-Set-1.zip," "Initial-Estimate-2026.pdf," "Storm-Report-March-2026.pdf," "Adjuster-Scope-Review.pdf," "Supplement-Request-1.pdf."]

The Documents tab is where every file related to this customer lives -- inspection photos, estimates, storm reports, adjuster correspondence, supplement requests, and contracts. You can download any file or upload new ones. When you receive a supplement request from the insurance company, upload it here so the entire team has access. When you prepare a documentation package for an adjuster meeting, everything you need is in one place.

[SCREEN: Click the "Tasks" tab. A list of tasks shows: "Adjuster Meeting -- Smith Property" (scheduled, Friday 2PM), "Submit Supplement #2" (pending, due Wednesday), "Follow up on claim denial" (overdue, in red). Each task shows title, type, status, dates, and assigned person.]

The Tasks tab tracks every action item on this claim. Adjuster meetings, supplement submissions, follow-up calls, re-inspections -- each one has a due date and an assigned person. If a task turns red, it is overdue. Use this to make sure nothing falls through the cracks during the back-and-forth of a claim.

[SCREEN: Open a new browser tab. Navigate to /check-my-address. Enter a customer's property address in the form: street, city, state, ZIP. Click "Get My Free Storm Report." A loading spinner appears, then the storm report renders showing a large "HIGH RISK" banner in orange, risk score 78/100, total hail reports: 14, closest hail: 2.3 miles, largest hail: 1.75 inches.]

Now let me show you the tool that makes your claims airtight. Go to Check My Address and enter the customer's property address. The system pulls real data from the National Weather Service and generates a comprehensive storm report. This one shows a High Risk score of 78 out of 100. There were 14 hail reports within 10 miles, the closest hail event was 2.3 miles away, and the largest hail recorded was 1.75 inches.

[SCREEN: Scroll down on the storm report. A timeline shows 10 recent hail events with columns for Date, Hail Size (inches), Severity (Moderate, Severe), and Distance (miles). Below that, a Risk Factors section with bullet points: "14 hail reports within 10 miles in 90 days," "Largest hail: 1.75 inches (threshold for damage: 1.0 inches)," "Closest event: 2.3 miles from property." Below that, a Recommendation section.]

Scroll down and you see the detailed timeline -- 10 recent hail events with exact dates, sizes in inches, severity ratings, and distances from the property. The Risk Factors section breaks down why the score is what it is. These are your talking points with the adjuster: "There were 14 documented hail events within 10 miles in the last 90 days. The largest hail was 1.75 inches, well above the 1-inch threshold for roof damage. The closest event was only 2.3 miles from the property." This is objective, third-party government weather data -- not our opinion. Adjusters respect this because it comes from the NWS, not from the roofing company. Save or screenshot this report and upload it to the customer's Documents tab so it is permanently attached to their record.

[SCREEN: Back on the customer detail page. Click a "Share Portal" or "Send Portal Link" button. A text message preview shows a unique customer portal URL. A brief view of the customer portal shows: job timeline, weather/hail data widget, messages, documents, delivery tracking, and appointments.]

You can also share the customer portal with the homeowner to keep them informed throughout the claims process. From the customer record, send them their unique portal link via text. The customer portal lets the homeowner see their job timeline, weather and hail data, messages, documents, delivery tracking, and upcoming appointments. This builds trust and reduces the "What's happening with my claim?" phone calls. When a homeowner can see their claim progressing in real time, they feel confident in the process.

[SCREEN: Navigate to /portal/schedule. A calendar view shows upcoming events. A new event is created: "Adjuster Meeting -- Johnson Property, Friday 2:00 PM." A Google Calendar link is generated.]

To schedule an adjuster meeting, go to the Schedule page and create the event. Set the date, time, and location. The system generates a Google Calendar link that you can share with the adjuster, the sales rep, and the homeowner. Add a note to the customer record with the meeting details so the full team knows when and where.

[SCREEN: Navigate to /portal/chat. A GroupMe conversation shows Bart messaging a sales rep: "Hey Brendon -- need the inspection photos from the Johnson property for the adjuster meeting Friday. Can you upload to their Documents tab?" Brendon replies: "Done -- 12 photos uploaded."]

Use GroupMe chat to coordinate with the team. If you need inspection photos from the sales rep who originally visited the property, message them directly. If the office needs to pull an invoice for a supplement, post in the group. If a claim gets denied and you need to strategize with leadership, send a DM. The chat keeps the team moving without waiting for email replies.

[SCREEN: The customer detail page with all six tabs visible, zoomed out to show the full interface.]

That is your insurance claims workflow. Pull up the customer record for the full six-tab detail view. Generate a storm report with Check My Address for objective NWS data. Upload every document to the customer's file. Schedule adjuster meetings through the calendar. Share the customer portal so homeowners can track their claim. Coordinate with the team through GroupMe. And document every conversation in the Notes tab. Every piece of evidence you need to support a claim is in this system -- the stronger your documentation, the faster claims get approved and the more supplements get paid.
