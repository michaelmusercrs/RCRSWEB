# VIDEO 07: Inventory Management -- Keeping RCRS Stocked and Ready

**Duration:** 5 minutes
**Audience:** Tae (Materials Manager)
**URL:** /portal/inventory

---

## NARRATION SCRIPT

[SCREEN: Browser opening rivercityroofingsolutions.com/portal. The login page loads. Email and PIN fields are filled in. After login, the portal dashboard appears. The user clicks into the inventory section.]

Welcome to Inventory Management. As Materials Manager, you are the person who makes sure no crew ever shows up to a job site without what they need. Everything flows through you -- receiving stock, verifying orders, coordinating with the driver, and keeping levels healthy. Let me show you the system that makes it all work.

[SCREEN: Browser showing /portal/inventory. The header reads "Inventory Management" with a package icon in green. Below the title: "142 items | 3 low stock" with the low stock count in amber. A Refresh button and a green "Add Item" button are on the right. A stats bar shows three cards: Total Items, Total Value (in dollars), and Low Stock Alerts with an amber triangle icon.]

This is your Inventory Dashboard. The header tells you two things immediately: how many items are in the system and how many are in low stock. The stats bar below breaks it down further -- Total Items is your full catalog count, Total Value shows the dollar value of everything on hand, and the Low Stock Alerts card turns amber when items need reordering. If that number is anything above zero, that is your first priority of the day.

[SCREEN: Three tabs below the stats bar -- "All Inventory" (active, package icon), "Low Stock" (warning triangle icon with amber count badge showing "3"), and "Transaction History" (clock icon). Below the tabs, a search bar with placeholder text "Search by name, SKU, supplier..." and a category filter dropdown showing "All Categories."]

You have three tabs to work with. All Inventory shows every product we track -- 11 categories covering shingles and bundles, underlayment, pipe boots, ridge caps, starter strips, drip edge, flashing, ice and water shield, ventilation, sealants, and fasteners. Low Stock filters to only the items below their reorder threshold -- check this tab first thing every morning. Transaction History is the complete audit trail showing every stock movement. Use the search bar to find any product by name, SKU, or supplier, and the category dropdown to filter by product type.

[SCREEN: The All Inventory tab showing a list of product rows. Each row displays: a stock level progress bar on the left, the product name in bold with a colored status badge (green "In Stock," amber "Low Stock," or red "Out of Stock"), SKU and category below the name, current quantity in large bold text on the right with the unit type and minimum threshold in parentheses, and two action buttons -- a bar chart icon for Quick Adjust and a pencil icon for Edit.]

The main inventory list shows every product at a glance. On the left, a small progress bar shows how full the stock is relative to the maximum. The product name has a color-coded status badge next to it. Green "In Stock" means the quantity is healthy. Amber "Low Stock" means you have hit or dropped below the minimum reorder threshold. Red "Out of Stock" means you are at zero -- that is urgent. Below the name you see the SKU, category, warehouse location, and supplier. On the right, the current quantity is displayed in large bold text with the unit type -- bundles, rolls, boxes, each -- and the minimum threshold in parentheses so you always know the target.

[SCREEN: Click the "Low Stock" tab. The list filters to show only three items with amber and red badges. One item shows "Pipe Boots" at 4 units with a minimum of 10. Another shows "Ice & Water Shield" at 0 rolls with a red "Out of Stock" badge.]

Click the Low Stock tab and you see only the items that need action. This morning there are three items flagged. Pipe Boots are at 4 units with a minimum of 10 -- that needs a reorder. Ice and Water Shield is at zero -- that is out of stock and urgent. For each of these, your next step is to contact the supplier, place the reorder, and then update the system when the stock arrives. If a crew needs one of these materials today and it is out of stock, message the office immediately through GroupMe so they can adjust the schedule.

[SCREEN: Click the bar chart Quick Adjust button on the "Pipe Boots" row. A panel slides down showing: plus and minus buttons around a quantity field, a "Reason" text input field, a preview line reading "New quantity: 54 each" in green, and green "Apply" and gray "Cancel" buttons.]

To adjust stock levels, click the Quick Adjust icon -- the bar chart button. A panel drops down with plus and minus buttons to set the quantity change and a Reason field where you describe why. For example, if ABC Supply just delivered 50 pipe boots, set the adjustment to +50 and type "Restock from ABC Supply -- PO #4821." The system previews the new quantity before you confirm. If the new quantity is still at or below the minimum, it shows in amber as a warning. Click Apply and the adjustment is saved. A transaction record is automatically created in the history.

[SCREEN: Click the expand arrow on a product row. The row expands to show a "Transaction History" section with a list of recent transactions. Green up arrows for restocks and returns, red down arrows for deliveries and pulls. Each entry shows date, type, reference number, quantity change, and who performed it. Below the transactions, four summary cards: Unit Cost, Total Value, Min/Max thresholds, and Warehouse Location.]

Click the expand arrow on any product to see its individual transaction history. Every movement is logged: green up arrows for restocks and returns, red down arrows for deliveries and material pulls. Each entry shows the date, the transaction type, a reference number, the quantity change, and who made it. Below the history, four summary cards show the Unit Cost, the Total Value of current stock, the Min/Max thresholds, and the warehouse Location. This is your audit trail -- if a number ever looks wrong, you can trace back exactly what happened and when.

[SCREEN: Click the "Transaction History" tab at the top. A full table loads with columns: Date, Item, Type (colored badges -- green "Restock," blue "Delivery," orange "Return," gray "Adjustment"), Qty Change (green positive numbers, red negative numbers), Reference, Value, and Status (Completed, Pending).]

The Transaction History tab gives you the complete picture across all products. Every stock movement across the entire inventory is here. Deliveries going out to job sites, restocks coming in from suppliers, returns, and manual adjustments -- all with dates, quantities, references, values, and statuses. When you need to reconcile at the end of the month or investigate a discrepancy, this is where you look.

[SCREEN: The office portal at /portal/office showing the "Create Order" tab. A material order form displays fields for job info, customer contact, delivery details (date, time, priority, special instructions), and a material selection grid. A line item shows "Shingles - Architectural, Qty: 45 bundles" with a running total at the bottom. A note reads: "Submitting this order will auto-create a delivery ticket."]

Here is how material orders connect to deliveries. When the PM or office staff creates a material order from the Office Portal, they select the products and quantities needed for a job. When they submit, the system automatically creates a delivery ticket and assigns it to the route. Your job is to verify that we have the requested materials in stock, pull them in the warehouse, and coordinate with Richard on loading. If anything is short, you flag it before it hits the loading checklist.

[SCREEN: The driver loading checklist at /portal/driver/loading showing material items grouped by delivery with checkboxes. Tae's name appears in a "Verified by" field at the bottom.]

The Loading Checklist is where your work meets the driver's work. Richard goes through each item on the checklist as he loads the truck, but you should verify the materials are staged correctly before he starts. Double-check the right products, the right quantities, and the right delivery groupings. If there is a substitution or shortage, flag it in GroupMe so the office can notify the customer before the truck leaves.

[SCREEN: Phone showing /portal/chat with a GroupMe conversation. A message from Tae reads: "Ice & Water Shield out of stock -- reorder placed with ABC Supply, ETA Thursday. Any jobs needing it before then?" A reply from John reads: "Johnson job needs 2 rolls by Wednesday -- can we get expedited?"]

Use GroupMe chat to coordinate with the team in real time. Message the PM about upcoming job material needs. Message the driver about loading priorities. Message the office about supplier delays or procurement needs. If something is out of stock and a job is affected, the sooner you communicate it, the sooner the team can adjust.

[SCREEN: The inventory dashboard scrolled to the top, showing the full stats bar and the beginning of the product list.]

That is your Inventory Management system. Every morning, check the Low Stock tab first. Process restocks with Quick Adjust as materials come in. Verify orders against stock before they go to loading. Keep the Transaction History clean so we always know what we have. You are the link between the supplier, the warehouse, and the job site -- when your numbers are right, everything else flows.
