# VIDEO 08: Insurance Claims -- Using RCRS Data for Every Claim

**Duration:** 5 minutes
**Audience:** Bart (Insurance Specialist)
**URL:** /portal/sales/customers

---

## NARRATION SCRIPT

[SCREEN: Browser opens /portal/sales/customers. A list of customers loads from JobNimbus CRM. Each row shows the customer name, phone, email, address, status, and the assigned sales rep. A search bar is at the top.]

Welcome to your insurance claims workflow. As the insurance specialist, you need fast access to customer records, storm data, and documentation. Let me show you how to pull everything together for a claim.

[SCREEN: Type a customer name in the search bar. The list filters down to one result. Click on the customer name.]

Start at the Customers page. This pulls directly from JobNimbus, our CRM. Use the search bar to find any customer by name, phone, email, or address. When you find the customer you need, click their name to open the full detail view.

[SCREEN: The customer detail page at /portal/sales/customers/[id] loads. At the top is the customer name, phone, email, address, status badge, and assigned rep. Below, six tabs are visible: Overview, Jobs, Estimates, Tasks, Notes, and Documents.]

The customer detail page has six tabs that give you everything about this customer. Let me walk through each one.

[SCREEN: The Overview tab is active. It shows the customer's full contact information -- name, phone numbers (mobile, home, work), email, full address, status, source, assigned sales rep, and dates for when the record was created and last updated.]

The Overview tab shows all the contact details. You have the customer's full name, every phone number on file -- mobile, home, and work -- their email, complete address with city, state, and ZIP, their current status in the pipeline, how they found us (the lead source), which sales rep is assigned, and when the record was created and last updated. When an adjuster asks for the homeowner's information, you pull it from here.

[SCREEN: Click the "Jobs" tab. A list of jobs for this customer appears, each showing the job number, name, description, status, address, sales rep, start date, and end date.]

The Jobs tab shows every job we have done or are doing for this customer. Each job has a number, name, description, status, the property address, which rep is handling it, and the start and end dates. For an insurance claim, you want to note the job status and the timeline -- when we inspected, when the work started, and when it was completed.

[SCREEN: Click the "Estimates" tab. A list of estimates shows estimate numbers, status badges, amounts, tax, total, description, and links to PDF and public URL.]

The Estimates tab shows every estimate we have written. You can see the estimate number, whether it has been approved or is pending, the line-item amount, tax, and total. There are links to download the PDF or open the public-facing version. When you are working with an adjuster, you can pull the estimate PDF directly from here and share it.

[SCREEN: Click the "Tasks" tab. A list of tasks shows titles, descriptions, types, status, start and end dates, and assigned team members.]

The Tasks tab lists all scheduled activities -- inspections, follow-up calls, meetings with adjusters, supplement submissions. Each task shows the title, description, type, status, dates, and who is assigned. Use this to track every touchpoint in the claims process.

[SCREEN: Click the "Notes" tab. A chronological list of notes appears with timestamps, author names, and note content.]

The Notes tab is your claims journal. Every note ever written about this customer is here in chronological order. When you have a call with an adjuster, add a note recording what was discussed, what documents were requested, and what the next step is. This creates a paper trail that protects RCRS if there is ever a dispute.

[SCREEN: Click the "Documents" tab. A list of uploaded documents shows file names, upload dates, and download links.]

The Documents tab stores every file attached to this customer -- photos, inspection reports, supplement documents, correspondence with the insurance company. You can download any file or upload new ones. When you get a supplement request from an adjuster, upload the document here so the whole team can access it.

[SCREEN: Open a new browser tab. Navigate to /check-my-address. The storm report form is shown.]

Now let me show you the storm data tool. Go to Check My Address -- this is the same tool customers use, but you can also use it internally. Enter the customer's property address, city, state, and ZIP.

[SCREEN: Fill in the address form and click "Get My Free Storm Report." A loading spinner appears, then the full storm report loads showing the risk level banner (e.g., "HIGH" in orange), risk score out of 100, total hail reports, closest hail distance, largest hail size, and a timeline of hail events with dates, sizes, severity, and distances.]

The system pulls real data from the National Weather Service and generates a storm report. This shows the risk level -- Low, Moderate, High, or Severe -- with a score out of 100. You see the total number of hail reports near the property, how close the nearest hail event was, and the largest hail size recorded. Below that is a timeline of specific hail events with dates, sizes in inches, severity ratings, and how far each event was from the property.

[SCREEN: Scroll to the Risk Factors section of the storm report, showing bullet points explaining why the risk is at its current level.]

The Risk Factors section explains exactly why the score is what it is. These are the talking points you use with an adjuster. For example: "There were 14 hail reports within 10 miles in the last 90 days. The largest recorded hail was 1.75 inches. The closest event was 2.3 miles away." This is objective, third-party weather data -- not our opinion. Adjusters respect this because it comes from the NWS.

[SCREEN: Scroll to the "Our Recommendation" section, then to the CTA with "Schedule Your Free Inspection" and phone number.]

The report includes a recommendation section that you can share with the homeowner to help them understand whether they should file a claim.

[SCREEN: Back on the customer detail page. Click the "Documents" tab. Click an "Upload" button and select a file.]

After generating the storm report, take a screenshot or save it and upload it to the customer's Documents tab. Now the storm data is permanently linked to their record. When you meet with the adjuster, you have the estimate, the storm report, inspection photos, and all notes in one place.

[SCREEN: On the customer detail page, hover over the "Portal" send button showing the customer portal link feature. A text message preview shows the portal URL.]

You can also share the customer portal with the homeowner. From the customer detail page or from the sales portal, tap "Portal" to send them a text with their unique portal link. The customer portal lets the homeowner see their job timeline, weather data, messages, documents, delivery tracking, and appointments. This keeps them informed and reduces phone calls asking for status updates.

[SCREEN: Navigate to /portal/schedule or show a Google Calendar link. An adjuster meeting is being scheduled.]

To schedule an adjuster meeting, go to the Schedule page and create the event. The system generates a Google Calendar link that you can share with the adjuster and the homeowner. Make sure to add a note on the customer record with the meeting date and time.

[SCREEN: Navigate to /portal/chat. A GroupMe conversation shows Bart coordinating with a sales rep about a claim.]

Use GroupMe to coordinate with the team. If the sales rep who originally inspected the property has photos or notes you need, message them directly. If the office needs to pull an invoice for a supplement, post it in the group chat. Everyone stays in the loop.

[SCREEN: The customer detail page with all six tabs visible, zoomed out.]

That is your insurance claims workflow. Search for the customer, review the six-tab detail view, pull a storm report for the property, upload documents, share the customer portal with the homeowner, schedule adjuster meetings through the calendar, and coordinate through GroupMe. Every piece of data you need to support a claim is in this system.
