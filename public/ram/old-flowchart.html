Replace Spark Plug #8 & Clean MAF Sensor
Now fix the immediate issues. This combo addresses 70% of misfire problems.
Spark Plug Replacement:
1. Install new spark plug (OEM Mopar Denso 3405 or Champion 3013)
2. Gap should be ~0.035" (check owner's manual)
3. Torque to 25-30 ft-lbs (use torque wrench if possible)
4. Reinstall coil pack on cylinder #8

MAF Sensor Cleaning:
1. Locate MAF sensor on intake duct (after air filter box)
2. Unplug electrical connector
3. Carefully unscrew sensor from housing
4. Spray with MAF CLEANER ONLY (NOT carb cleaner)
5. Let dry completely (don't blow dry)
6. Reinstall and plug in connector

Ensure tank is FULL before testing

9
Coil Swap Test (Diagnose if Coil is Bad)
Next most likely culprit: Ignition coil pack #8 is weak or failing (20% probability).
What you're doing:
Swap the coil pack from cylinder #8 to another cylinder. If the misfire FOLLOWS the coil, the coil is bad. If it stays on #8, something else is wrong.

Steps:
1. Remove coil pack #8 (unscrew bolt, unplug connector)
2. Remove coil pack #1 (easiest access, front right)
3. Install coil #8 onto cylinder #1 hole
4. Install coil #1 onto cylinder #8 hole
5. Start engine, listen and feel for vibration
6. If vibration/misfire moves to cylinder #1 = BAD COIL #8
7. If vibration stays on cylinder #8 = NOT the coil (might be injector)

Swap back the coils to original position when done.
$0 (test only)
15 min
⬇️
10
⚠️ DECISION: Did the misfire follow the coil?
YES: Misfire moved to Cyl #1
Coil #8 is BAD
NO: Misfire stayed on Cyl #8
It's not the coil
✓ If misfire FOLLOWED the coil: Ignition coil #8 is faulty. Order replacement (Mopar #68305517AA or equivalent). Cost: $25-120. Installation: 30 min. This is your fix (20% probability).
✗ If misfire STAYED on Cyl #8: Not the coil. Problem is likely fuel injector or mechanical (compression). Proceed to Step 11 (Compression Test).

11
Compression Test on Cylinder #8 (Rule Out Mechanical)
Last diagnostic before considering fuel injector or shop visit. Rules out low compression (bad valve, worn ring).
What you need:
Compression gauge (loaner from auto parts, $0) or buy for $30-50

Steps:
1. Warm engine to normal operating temp
2. Remove spark plugs from cylinders #8 AND two others (for comparison)
3. Screw compression gauge into cylinder #8
4. Have helper crank engine 4-5 times (you hold throttle WIDE OPEN)
5. Read pressure on gauge
6. Repeat for cylinders #7 and #4 (for comparison)

What the numbers mean:
• Cylinder #8 >140 psi AND within 10% of others = ✓ GOOD (compression is healthy)
• Cylinder #8 <120 psi OR >15% lower than others = ✗ BAD (mechanical problem)
$0-50 (gauge)
1 hour
⬇️
12
⚠️ DECISION: What's the compression reading?
Compare cylinder #8 to cylinders #7 and #4:
Is #8 >140 psi and within 10% of the others?
ABOVE 140 PSI & within 10% of others?
Compression is GOOD
BELOW 120 PSI or >15% lower?
Mechanical problem detected
✓ If compression is GOOD (>140 psi): Cylinder #8 is mechanically healthy. Problem is likely FUEL INJECTOR (stuck open/clogged). Next step: Professional fuel injector cleaning ($150-300) or shop diagnostics.
✗ If compression is LOW (<120 psi): Cylinder #8 has a mechanical failure (burnt valve, damaged ring, or bent piston). This is expensive. Options:
1. Valve job (~$1,500-2,500 shop labor)
2. Used/reman engine (~$2,000-3,500)
Consider the truck's overall value. At 320k miles, weigh repair cost vs replacement.
