# RCRS Customer Service & Communication Guide

## Customer Portal Overview

The RCRS Customer Portal gives homeowners visibility into their project:

- **URL**: /customer-portal (accessed via unique token link)
- **Job Timeline**: Visual progress from inspection to completion
- **Weather & Hail Data**: Storm history for their address
- **Messages**: Direct communication channel with their rep and office
- **Documents**: Access to contracts, estimates, invoices, and warranty info
- **Delivery Tracking**: Real-time status of material deliveries
- **Appointments**: View and request schedule changes

### Directing Customers to the Portal
- Provide the portal link at contract signing
- Walk them through the key features
- Remind them it's available 24/7 for checking status
- Use it to reduce inbound "where's my project?" calls

## Communication Standards

Every interaction with a customer reflects on RCRS. Maintain professionalism:

### Response Time Targets

| Channel | Target Response Time | Maximum |
|---------|---------------------|---------|
| Phone (business hours) | 15 minutes | 30 minutes |
| Text/SMS | 1 hour | 2 hours |
| Email | 2 hours | 4 hours |
| Portal message | 2 hours | 4 hours |
| After-hours urgent | Next business morning | 9 AM |

### Communication Best Practices
- **Be proactive**: Don't wait for customers to ask for updates -- provide them
- **Be specific**: "Your materials will be delivered Tuesday between 9-11 AM" not "soon"
- **Be honest**: If there's a delay, communicate it immediately with a new timeline
- **Be professional**: Use proper grammar, avoid slang, and maintain a friendly tone
- **Document everything**: Log all communications in JN notes

## Insurance Terminology

Understanding insurance terms helps you communicate confidently with homeowners:

### Key Terms
- **RCV (Replacement Cost Value)**: Full cost to replace with new materials, no depreciation deducted
- **ACV (Actual Cash Value)**: RCV minus depreciation -- the initial insurance payout
- **Deductible**: Homeowner's out-of-pocket cost before insurance pays (typically $1,000-$2,500)
- **Recoverable depreciation**: Depreciation amount paid back after work is completed
- **Supplement**: Additional items found after initial estimate, filed as an add-on to the claim
- **O&P (Overhead & Profit)**: Standard 20% markup when a general contractor manages the project
- **Adjuster**: Insurance company representative who inspects damage and determines payout
- **Scope of loss**: Detailed list of damaged items and required repairs

### Explaining the Process to Homeowners
1. "We inspect your roof for storm damage at no cost"
2. "If we find damage, we help you file an insurance claim"
3. "The insurance company sends an adjuster to verify"
4. "Once approved, you pay only your deductible -- insurance covers the rest"
5. "We handle all the paperwork and coordination"

## Handling Objections

Common objections and recommended responses:

### "Your price is too high"
- Acknowledge: "I understand cost is a concern"
- Explain value: quality materials, manufacturer warranty, RCRS workmanship guarantee
- Compare: cost of delaying repairs (water damage, mold, structural issues)
- Insurance: if insurance is paying, the homeowner only pays the deductible

### "I need to think about it"
- Respect their time: "Absolutely, this is an important decision"
- Ask: "What specific questions do I need to answer to help you decide?"
- Provide timeline: "Keep in mind the insurance filing deadline is [date]"
- Schedule: "Can I follow up with you on [specific day]?"

### "I already have a roofer"
- Acknowledge: "That's great that you have someone you trust"
- Differentiate: "We specialize in insurance claims, which can be complex"
- Offer: "Would a second opinion on the scope be valuable?"
- Leave: business card and door hanger for future reference

### "I don't think I have damage"
- Educate: "Hail damage isn't always visible from the ground"
- Offer: "A free inspection takes 20 minutes and gives you peace of mind"
- Evidence: "We found damage on [X] homes in your neighborhood this week"
- No pressure: "If there's no damage, we'll tell you and be on our way"

### "The insurance company denied my claim"
- Don't give up: "Denials can often be appealed or re-inspected"
- Offer: "We can request a re-inspection and be present to point out damage"
- Document: "We'll prepare detailed documentation to support the appeal"
- Set expectations: "The re-inspection process typically takes 2-3 weeks"

## Document Management

Customers should receive the right documents at the right time:

### At Contract Signing
- Signed contract copy
- Scope of work document
- Insurance claim information (claim number, adjuster contact)
- Expected project timeline
- Portal access instructions

### During the Project
- Material delivery notifications
- Schedule updates
- Any change orders or supplements

### At Completion
- Final invoice
- Warranty documentation (manufacturer + RCRS workmanship)
- Maintenance recommendations
- Referral information

## Referral Program

Referrals are the highest-quality lead source. Make asking systematic:

### When to Ask
- After the customer confirms satisfaction with the completed work
- Typically 24-48 hours after job completion
- During the 30-day follow-up call

### How to Ask
- "We're so glad you're happy with the work"
- "Do you know anyone -- a neighbor, friend, or family member -- who might need a roof inspection?"
- "We'd love to take care of them the same way we took care of you"

### Making It Easy
- Share the referral page link (on the website)
- Provide referral cards the customer can hand out
- Explain the referral bonus (if applicable)
- Follow up with a thank-you when their referral converts

## Follow-Up Schedule

Consistent follow-up builds relationships and generates referrals:

### Post-Completion
- **24-48 hours**: Call to confirm satisfaction, ask if they have questions
- **1 week**: Check if any issues have come up
- **30 days**: Follow-up call, ask for referral, request Google review
- **6 months**: Check-in, seasonal maintenance reminder
- **Annually**: Anniversary check-in, offer maintenance inspection

### Follow-Up Best Practices
- Log every follow-up in JN notes
- Set reminders in TeamUp calendar
- Personalize each contact -- reference their specific job
- Be genuinely helpful, not just looking for referrals

## Escalation Process

Know when and how to escalate:

### When to Escalate
- Customer requests to speak with a manager
- Complaint cannot be resolved at your level
- Safety concern reported
- Decision exceeds your authority (pricing, scope changes, refunds)
- Legal threat or mention of attorney

### How to Escalate
1. Document the full situation in JN notes
2. Notify your manager immediately (call or GroupMe DM)
3. Provide the manager with: customer name, job number, issue summary, what you've tried
4. Follow up to ensure the customer received a response
