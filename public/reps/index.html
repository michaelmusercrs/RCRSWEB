Read first These figures are commission, not profit

The two source reports carry revenue and sales commission — nothing else. Neither one contains materials, crew labor, or overhead attributable to a rep. Crew pay does appear in the 1099 report (account 5800, about $7.9M), but it is booked by crew vendor with memos like CONTRACTOR PAY W.E. 5.7.2026 — no job or customer reference — so it cannot be traced back to a rep.

Everything below therefore measures revenue less commission, which runs 85–90%. Real gross margin on a roof is typically 25–40%. To get there you need a job-costing export (P&L by Customer/Job, or Sales by Rep with COGS).

§2

The blended commission rate barely moves

Across nine years and $36M of revenue, the company-wide rate stayed inside a four-point band. That stability is the strongest signal in this data — it means the outliers in §4 are worth a look, because the baseline is genuinely consistent.

§3

Who produced it

Eight reps account for the large majority of nine years of revenue. Hunter Rivers alone invoiced $6.29M — more than the bottom twenty-two reps combined.

§4

Every established producer sits between 11% and 14%. The reps outside that band are the ones worth a conversation, since the baseline is tight enough that a gap of four points is real rather than noise.

§7

What to distrust, and why

Four things in this data will mislead anyone reading a single cell without context. Each one is mechanical, and each one is fixable with a different export.

Resolved Three reps are paid through an LLC

$804K of commission is paid to names that match no rep on the invoice report. Three of those payees — $724K, the large majority — were confirmed by the owner on August 10, 2026 and are folded into the rep totals everywhere in this report. In each case the rep's personal commission stops in the same year the entity's begins, and folding them in pulls all three back into the normal 11–14% band.

  • BCM Contracting LLC → Brendon Muse
  • Roof Angel, LLC → Aaron Lussi
  • Rudys Roofing Insights LLC → Adam Rudell

Open Nine more payees are still unassigned

Roughly $80K of commission, all between 2023 and 2025, sits with payees — six LLCs and three individuals — that nobody has matched to a rep. It is not included in any rep total, so the rates in §4 for whoever earned it are understated.

Artefact Eight reps show a 100% margin

MJ Muse, Sean Newberry, Dustin Shariett, Steve Cofield, Jacob Hill, Jason Cagle, Boston Muse and Tawny Haga have revenue but no 1099 record at all. They were on W-2 payroll, which a 1099 report by definition excludes. Their commission is real — it just isn't in this file. They carry a ⚠ No 1099 data flag in the matrix.

Artefact John Cordonis is not a producing rep

He received $767K in commission against $607K of his own invoices — a 126% rate. He draws override and division-leader pay on other reps' jobs, so his line measures something different from everyone else's and should never be compared directly. He carries an Override pay flag.

Timing Payment lag inflates the newest column

Commission is dated by check; revenue is dated by invoice. Checks routinely clear weeks after the job invoices, so a partial year shows commission against revenue that has not fully landed. That is the whole explanation for Adam Rudell at 31.5% in 2026, Brendon Muse at 26.8%, and Richard Geahr at 26.2% — not raises. The same effect runs backwards for a departing rep: Ryan Butcher's 2023 rate is commission trailing a $2,000 residual year.

Excluded Non-job invoice groups

About $28.8K across BOUNCED CHECK, CANCELLED JOB, CXL JOB, VOIDED INVOICE, DOES NOT GET COUNTED IN NUMBERS and Not Specified is excluded from every figure here. Most of it is 2021.
